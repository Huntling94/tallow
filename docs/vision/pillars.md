# Pillars

Three pillars decide what goes in the game. Every feature issue names the pillar it serves. If a feature serves none, it does not go in.

## 1. Steer the herd

Layered defences protect the town and bring the beasts down; your townspeople turn the fallen into wealth. How you split money between stopping the herd and harvesting it decides what you take home.

- **The test:** does this change where the herd goes, what it costs to stop it, or what your people can take from it?
- **Serves it:** a new trap that turns beasts without killing them; a terrain feature that changes route odds; a wagon that lets crews reach far carcasses before they spoil.
- **Does not:** a decorative tower with no effect on routes or harvest.

## 2. Read the odds

Every beast, buyer and rival runs on visible probabilities. Better forecasts make you rich.

- **The test:** does this give the player a probability to read, act on, or improve?
- **Serves it:** the almanac's confidence bands; price that responds to supply; a rumour that narrows a forecast; a rare stampede whose odds are shown, so the player can choose whether to pay to prepare for it.
- **Does not:** a random event whose odds were never shown, so the player could not have prepared for it. Rare events belong in the game; hidden ones do not.

## 3. Live in what you built

Your house, the shops, the exchange floor and the skyline you paid for, walkable in first person.

- **The test:** does walking the town change a decision or show the player what they achieved?
- **Serves it:** the best exchange deals only on the floor in person; your house growing with your wealth.
- **Does not:** a first-person errand that duplicates a menu with no new choice.

## When pillars conflict

Steer the herd wins over Read the odds, which wins over Live in what you built. The defence and the odds are the game; the town walk is what makes it yours. Players should feel the statistics through tension and prices, not through spreadsheets.
